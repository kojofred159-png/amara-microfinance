The problem

Most Kenyan MFIs inherit a group lending model — few ever redesign it

Group lending policy is usually copied from a founding template and left untouched as the institution grows. What worked for 3 branches under close supervision quietly breaks down at 15, and portfolio-at-risk becomes a lagging symptom of a design problem no one is diagnosing.

Symptom

PAR creeps up branch by branch

Group guarantees weaken as centres grow past their original size, group formation rules aren't enforced consistently, and loan officers are left to patch the gaps manually.

Root cause

The policy was never built for scale

Group size, meeting cadence, guarantor liability, and top-up rules are usually set once at launch and rarely revisited against what field data is actually showing.

The loan cycle

Every fix traces back to one of five stages

Group lending breaks down at a specific, identifiable stage. We start by locating which one, rather than redesigning what already works.

01 — FORMATION

Group formation

Who joins, group size limits, and screening for repayment capacity before a group is registered.

02 — TRAINING

Pre-disbursement training

Group members understand liability, meeting rules, and consequences of default before money moves.

03 — DISBURSEMENT

Loan disbursement

Approval limits, loan sizing against group savings, and turnaround time from application to payout.

04 — REPAYMENT

Repayment & guarantee

Centre meeting discipline, guarantor enforcement, and how early arrears are actually handled in the field.

05 — RENEWAL

Review & renewal

Loan sizing for the next cycle, graduation rules, and whether the group structure should change as members mature.

Grounded in regulation

Built around how CBK actually regulates microfinance

Recommendations are written to sit inside the Microfinance Act and CBK prudential expectations for deposit-taking and credit-only institutions — not as generic best practice imported from elsewhere.

  • 01 Policy recommendations reference current CBK prudential guidelines for the relevant licence class.
  • 02 Group lending redesigns account for CBK reporting and disclosure obligations, not just portfolio mechanics.
  • 03 Engagements are scoped around Kenya's MFI landscape — branch structures, agent networks, and the realities of field collection.
  • 04 Where a recommendation touches licensing or capital requirements, we flag it clearly rather than treating it as settled.
